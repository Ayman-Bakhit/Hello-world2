# Ag/Food B2B Opportunity Scan: Decision Memo

Prepared 2026-11-23. Founder constraints: solo, $1,000 cash, 60 hrs/week, no equipment, certifications or contacts.

## 1. Decision and confidence

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE. Run one cheap test (about $0 to $60, 14 days). Do not build software and do not spend the $1,000 until the test passes.**

- Best candidate: a **"cut-sheet and booking desk" for small meat lockers**. You chase each locker's customers for cutting instructions and appointment confirmations by phone, text and an online form. That way the owner stops losing hours to it and stops eating no-shows.
- Testable without touching food or machinery: **yes**. All the work is admin and phone.
- Confidence that the problem exists: **medium**. Public locker pages show the workflow: phone/email/PDF cut sheets, 7-day deadlines and cancellation fees.
- Confidence that lockers will pay an outsider for it: **low, and unproven**. Nothing found shows willingness to pay. One Iowa locker has already solved it with an online form, and software that covers cut sheets already exists from $99/month.
- **Fit with your goals: poor as a wealth engine.** Even 50 paying lockers at $150/month comes to about $90k/year of revenue. Treat this as a fast, cheap lesson in B2B selling, not as your $30M+ vehicle.

The report's own definitions explain who is in the "Other" bucket:
- NFI plants are "State-inspected or custom-exempt" ("about 1,800").
- Custom-exempt plants "do not sell meat but operate on a custom basis only."
- Mobile slaughter units are excluded.

**Estimated core target pool: about 1,800 to 2,500 plants.** That is the 1,796 NFI plants plus up to 677 small FI cattle plants. Some small FI plants may also count under hogs, so treat 2,473 as an upper bound.
- This is a count of establishments, **not of budgets**. Nothing in the count shows any plant pays for admin help.
- Excluded: processing-only shops that don't slaughter (not counted by NASS), and on-farm/mobile slaughter.

- **Why this one:** its process is manual, with PDF cut sheets returned by email, phone or drop-off. It has hard deadlines and a cancellation fee. That is the exact workflow you would take over.
- **What is NOT proven:** budget, willingness to pay, and whether this owner even sees it as a problem. A website is not a budget. The interview below exists to find out.
- **Counter-example to call too: Milo Locker Meats**, Milo, IA, (641) 942-6231 (Verified on site). It already runs an online cut-order form with deposit checkout. Ask the owner whether it cut phone time and what it cost. If it fully solved the problem for little money, that weakens the case for paying you.

## 6. Buyer interview (six questions, owner or office manager, 15 min)

Opener: "I'm not selling anything today. I'm researching how small lockers handle cut sheets and booking. Can I ask six quick questions?"

1. "Walk me through the last beef you processed that was split between several of a farmer's customers. From drop-off to the last cut sheet being in, who called whom, how many times, and how long did it take?"
2. "In a normal week in your busy season, how many hours do you or your staff spend chasing cut sheets, confirming drop-off dates, or calling people to pick up? Who does it, and what would they be doing instead?"
3. "In the last 12 months, how many animals came in without a cut sheet by your deadline, and what did you do: cut to your standard, hold the carcass, or call again? What did that cost you in cooler space or redo time?"
4. "How many booked appointments were cancelled or no-showed in the last year? Did you actually collect the cancellation fee, and could you fill the slot?"
5. "Have you tried an online form, software like Cleaver or Finer Butcher, or hiring office help for this? What happened, and why did you keep it or drop it?"
6. "If someone took over every cut-sheet chase and appointment-confirmation call for your next 30 days of beef and hogs, working from your own forms and calendar, what would that be worth to you? Would you pay $300 up front for a 30-day trial starting with your January bookings?"

Scoring: count the hours given in Q2, the misses in Q3 and Q4, and whether Q6 is a yes, a no, or "maybe later". **"Maybe later" counts as a no.**

## 9. Gating risks and strongest contrary evidence

**Strongest contrary evidence:**
- **Milo Locker Meats already solved cut sheets with a free-to-cheap online form plus deposit checkout.**
- **Cleaver sells cut-sheet software from $99/mo.**

If the fix is a form the locker can build in an afternoon, your service is a commodity.

**Other risks:**
- Lockers are capacity-constrained, so the bottleneck may be labor and cooler space, not paperwork.
- Owners are cash-tight and phone-first.
- Customer data and texting consent belong to the locker.
- The busy season makes it hard to get meetings now.
- The idea doesn't scale to wealth.
- You have no meat-industry credibility. Mitigation: listen more than you pitch.

## 10. Cheapest test and kill criterion

- **Test:** contact 40 lockers and complete interviews with 15 owners or managers within 14 days. Cost is $0 by phone and email, about $60 if you add 1–2 nearby walk-ins.
- **Kill if any one is true by day 14:**
  - Fewer than 6 of 15 report ≥2 hours/week on cut-sheet or booking chasing.
  - **Fewer than 2 agree to a prepaid pilot of ≥$200.**
  - More than 8 of 15 already use an online form or software and say it works.
- **Continue only if** ≥2 prepaid pilots close. Then the next gate is ≥1 of the 2 converting to $150/mo after 30 days.
