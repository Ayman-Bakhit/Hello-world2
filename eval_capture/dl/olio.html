Olio Vintage Expands

The vintage collective's new, supersized store brings more dealers, local makers, art, and room to gather under one roof.

by Madeline Nachbar

Style

November 16, 2026

8:54 AM
RSS
Print
×
Expand

Olio Vintage, the Northeast Minneapolis vintage collective founded by longtime vintage dealer Carrie Martinson in 2022, has relocated from its original 5th Street storefront to a historic 1900 building just around the corner. At approximately 5,000 square feet, the new digs are more than triple the space of the original shop, making room for more vintage vendors, local artists, and community programming.

×
Expand

For Martinson, the move was a long time coming. “Space has been an issue for some time now,” she says. “We have had so many vendors asking to join us and we just had nowhere to put them.”

Olio's signature shop-within-a-shop concept remains intact, with independent vintage dealers curating their own distinct assortments of clothing, accessories, and homewares. Returning favorites include Rosella Vintage, Black Nvni, Lobstergarden, Oubliette, and Renegade Rags, while newcomers such as Glam Diggers Vintage, Weekend Salvage, Backstitch Vintage, and Siempre Viva Vintage join the lineup. The larger footprint also allows for vintage furniture and a rotating roster of local artists and makers.

×
Expand

One particularly noteworthy addition is a glass-enclosed showroom near the entrance, which Martinson describes as “our version of Dayton's Oval Room.” Inside, shoppers will find an edited selection of standout vintage designer clothing, fur coats, handbags, jewelry, and other special pieces.

Martinson's vision extends well beyond retail. Lounge areas invite shoppers to linger, while flexible event space opens the door to workshops, pop-ups, and creative collaborations.

“I envision everything from visible mending and floral arranging to auto maintenance and mental health workshops,” she says. “We have such a talented community of artists and makers, and being able to bring everyone together in this space is a dream come true.” A café is on her wish list for the future, too.

“I want a place where the community can gather and be inspired and collaborate on projects,” Martinson adds. “Fashion, art and vintage clothing are so connected and I want to really be a place to foster that connection and give upcoming talent a place to showcase their art.” 25 NE 4th St., Mpls.
