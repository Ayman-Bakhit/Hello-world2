Prognostic Discussion for Long-Lead Seasonal Outlooks 
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park MD 
830 AM EST Thu Nov 19 2026 
 
SUMMARY OF THE OUTLOOK FOR NON-TECHNICAL USERS 
 
A strong El Nino continues and is forecast to persist through 
January-February-March 2027. The strong El Nino is likely to be the major 
influence on temperatures and precipitation this winter through next spring. 
 
The Temperature Outlook for December-January-February (DJF) 2026-2027 favors 
above-normal temperatures for central to eastern Alaska along with much of the 
northern two-thirds of the Contiguous U.S. (CONUS). Above-normal temperature 
probabilities exceed 50% across the Northeast, Great Lakes, Upper Mississippi 
Valley, Pacific Northwest, and southeastern Alaska. Near-normal temperatures, 
averaged over the three-month period, are favored for parts of the Southeast. 
 
The DJF Precipitation Outlook depicts an increased chance of above-normal 
precipitation for southwestern Oregon, California, the Southwest, Central to 
Southern Great Plains, Southeast, Mid-Atlantic, and much of the Northeast. The 
largest above-normal precipitation probabilities (> 70% chance) are forecast 
across Florida, southern Georgia, and the coastal Carolinas. Below-normal 
precipitation is more likely for parts of the Great Lakes, Northern High 
Plains, Northern Rockies, and Mainland Alaska. 
 
Equal chances (EC, white areas) are forecast for areas where probabilities for 
each category (below, near, or above) of three-month mean temperatures and 
accumulated precipitation amounts are forecast to be similar to climatological 
probabilities. 
 
BASIS AND SUMMARY OF THE CURRENT LONG-LEAD OUTLOOKS 
Note: For Graphical Displays of the Forecast Tools Discussed Below See: 
http://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/predictions/90day/tools/briefing 
 
CURRENT ATMOSPHERIC AND OCEANIC CONDITIONS 
 
As of mid-November, weekly observed sea surface temperature (SST) departures in 
the Nino 3.4 region are +2.2 degrees C with equatorial SSTs above average 
across the east-central Pacific Ocean. Upper-ocean (0 to 300 meters) anomalies 
from the Date Line to 100W are near +2.5 degrees C but have decreased slightly 
since peaking in September. The Relative Oceanic Nino Index for 
August-September-October was +2.0 degrees C, just shy of +2.1 degrees C in 
1997. From mid-October to mid-November, below-average OLR anomalies (enhanced 
convection and precipitation) were observed across the east-central equatorial 
Pacific with above-average OLR anomalies (suppressed convection and 
precipitation) across Indonesia and the Maritime Continent. Low-level and 
upper-level (850-hPa and 200-hPa) wind anomalies were westerly and easterly, 
respectively, from 160E to 120W. The oceanic and atmospheric observations 
reflect an ongoing strong El Nino. 
 
Any Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO) signal continues to be overwhelmed and 
masked by the strong El Nino. Upper-level velocity potential anomaly 
observations depict a MJO that propagated east to the Indian Ocean by 
mid-November. Dynamical model forecasts indicate that this MJO may propagate 
east to the Western Hemisphere by the beginning of December. However, the MJO 
is unlikely to have much influence on the mid-latitude circulation pattern 
given the strength of the El Nino. 
 
PROGNOSTIC DISCUSSION OF SST FORECASTS 
 
As of November 12, the NOAA outlook indicates that El Nino is likely to persist 
through MAM 2027 with a transition to ENSO-neutral conditions becoming more 
probable by AMJ. The SST consolidation forecast for Nino 3.4 depicts SST 
anomalies peaking above +2.5 degrees C during NDJ 2026-27 and decreasing later 
in the winter and into the spring. By the 2027 fall and 2027-28 winter, this 
prognostic tool depicts a weak to moderate La Nina with SST anomalies between 
-0.5 and -1.0 degrees C. 
 
PROGNOSTIC TOOLS USED FOR U.S. TEMPERATURE AND PRECIPITATION OUTLOOKS 
 
The Temperature and Precipitation Outlooks from DJF 2026-27 through AMJ 2027 
were based on: dynamical models  such as the North American Multi-Model Ensemble 
(NMME) and the International Multi-Model Ensemble (IMME) along with an 
objective, historical skill-weighted consolidation that includes the NMME along 
with statistical tools such as the SST-based Constructed Analog and the 
ENSO-OCN. Also, for DJF 2026-27, observed temperatures and precipitation during 
stronger El Ninos (since 1997-98) and strong/very strong El Nino composites 
were heavily weighed. Beginning in MJJ and through OND 2027, ENSO-OCN 
(combination of ENSO and long-term trend) was the primary tool used. During NDJ 
and DJF 2027-28, the outlooks incorporated La Nina composites. 
 
PROGNOSTIC DISCUSSION OF OUTLOOKS - DJF 2026 TO DJF 2027 
 
TEMPERATURE 
 
The historically strong El Nino is forecast to be the major influence on the 
mid-latitude circulation pattern and temperatures this winter. Typically during 
El Nino winters, DJF temperatures average above normal for most of the northern 
two-thirds of the CONUS with three-month temperatures more variable and 
averaging either below, near, or above normal for the southern third of the 
CONUS. The DJF 2026-27 outlook closely followed El Nino composites and the 
latest dynamical model output (NMME and IMME). Above-normal temperature 
probabilities exceed 50% for much of the Northeast, Great Lakes, Upper 
Mississippi Valley, Pacific Northwest, and southeastern Alaska. In addition, 
these probabilities are largest (> 60%) from northwestern Oregon and Washington 
northward to southeastern Alaska due to the latest dynamical model output and 
strong/very strong El Nino composites. 
 
Forecast uncertainty is higher for the Southwest, Southern Great Plains, Gulf 
Coast, and Southeast during the 2026-27 winter through the 2027 spring. Without 
a major shift in any of the model guidance, no changes were made to these areas 
from the previous outlook. In the more recent stronger El Ninos (2023-24, 
2015-16, and 1997-98), temperatures varied largely over the Southwest where 
equal chances (EC) of below, near, or above-normal temperature are forecast. 
During the 2023-24 and 1997-98 winters, DJF temperatures verified in the middle 
tercile for most of Florida and from southeastern Louisiana east to southern 
Georgia. Therefore, the DJF 2026-27 temperature outlook leans towards near 
normal temperatures being the most likely outcome for that part of the 
Southeast. The DJF temperature observations for 2009-10 (stronger El Nino) was 
heavily influenced by a persistent and highly negative Arctic Oscillation (AO) 
with the following monthly values: Dec: -3.4, Jan: -2.6, Feb: -4.3. Although 
the 2009-10 winter outcome with a broad coverage of below-normal temperatures 
across the Great Plains and Southeast can not be completely ruled out, it is 
the least likely one among the more recent stronger El Ninos. The primary 
source of uncertainty in the temperature outlook is how much high latitude 
blocking prevails this winter. The AO and North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) was 
in a positive phase from mid-September through October, but became negative in 
early November. The GEFS and ECMWF ensemble means favor a -NAO heading into 
December. A -NAO would tend to result in below-normal temperatures across the 
Southeast but there is high uncertainty on the NAO phase through the 2026-27 
winter. 
 
El Nino impacts are expected to persist through at least the spring 2027 and 
the temperature outlooks reflect this. The outlooks from MJJ through OND 2027 
are based primarily on the ENSO-OCN forecast tool which is derived from the CPC 
SST consolidation (ENSO component) and long term temperature trends  (Optimal 
Climate Normal). Historically, La Nina follows a strong El Nino and given this 
likelihood, the NDJ and DJF 2027-28 outlooks were based more on typical La Nina 
influences. 
 
PRECIPITATION 
 
Due to the historically strong El Nino and its reliable wet pattern for a 
majority of the CONUS, there is higher forecast confidence in the precipitation 
than the temperature outlook this winter. For DJF 2026-27, a predicted active 
southern stream along with enhanced subtropical moisture leads to a large 
coverage of greater than a 40% chance of above-normal precipitation from 
southwestern Oregon and California east through the Southwest, Central to 
Southern Great Plains, Gulf Coast, and much of the East Coast. Based on the 
latest dynamical model output, above-normal precipitation probabilities were 
increased across California, the Desert Southwest, and along the East Coast 
compared to the previous forecast release on October 15. In southern California 
and southwestern Arizona, probabilities exceed 60% while greater than a 70% 
chance of above-normal precipitation is forecast for Florida, southern Georgia, 
and the coastal Carolinas. The elevated above-normal precipitation probabilties 
extend north to the western Corn Belt and also along the West Coast to Oregon 
given the good model agreement and strong/very strong El Nino composites. 
Excellent model consistency the past few months along with strong/very strong 
El Nino composites support below-normal precipitation probabilities exceeding 
50% across the northern Rockies and much of Mainland Alaska. Consistent with 
the El Nino response and its southward shift in the storm track, elevated 
below-normal precipitation probabilities are forecast for parts of the Great 
Lakes and Northern Great Plains. The Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO) has 
remained negative throughout the 2020s. Although the October 2026 PDO value was 
negative, positive SST anomalies have diminished over the northeastern Pacific 
due to fall storminess. It is uncertain if the -PDO persists through the 
2026-27 winter, and regardless, it is likely that the historically strong El 
Nino will be the major influence on the precipitation pattern across the Lower 
48 states. 
 
El Nino impacts typically peak during the late winter across the Southeast, 
which supports maintaining large above-normal precipitation probabilities (> 
70%) for that region through JFM. Since El Nino impacts are expected to linger 
into the spring, there is greater than a 40% chance of above-normal 
precipitation across much of California along with parts of the Southwest and 
Southeast during FMA and MAM. The precipitation outlooks from MJJ through OND 
2027 are based on the ENSO-OCN forecast tool which is from the CPC SST 
consolidation (ENSO component) and long term temperature trends  (Optimal 
Climate Normal). Historically, La Nina follows a strong El Nino and given this 
likelihood, the NDJ and DJF 2027-28 outlooks were based more on La Nina 
influences.
