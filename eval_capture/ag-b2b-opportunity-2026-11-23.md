# U.S. Ag / Food-Production B2B Opportunity Screen

Date: 2026-11-23. Constraints: solo founder, $1,000, 60 h/week, no equipment, certifications or contacts. Must be testable without handling food or operating machinery.

## 1. Decision (confidence first)

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE, lean NO-GO on committing real money. Run one capped test only.**

- Best candidate found: **"missing cut-sheet and overdue-pickup chaser" for very small USDA-inspected meat processors** (remote, phone/text only, no food contact).
- Confidence it is a real *paid* business: **low (~20 to 30%, judgment, not computed).** Evidence that the problem exists is decent. Evidence that processors will pay an outsider to fix it is **zero** so far.
- Even if it works, it is a labor business: modeled ceiling is about **$5.5k/month contribution** for one founder (Section 7). It is a cash and learning wedge, not a wealth engine, unless it later turns into software, where funded competitors already exist.
- Spend **$60 or less**, take **10 working days**, then kill or continue using the numbers in Section 9.

## 5. Selected problem and offer

**Problem:** At very small plants, cutting instructions arrive late or not at all, and finished orders sit in the freezer past pickup. Processors publish fallback rules such as "If no cut sheet has been submitted, we will cut to the Butcher's recommendation" ([Western Heritage Meat Co. cut-sheet page](https://westernheritagemeatco.squarespace.com/cut-sheets), verified). That shows the gap exists. It also shows plants push the cost onto the producer (see Section 10).

**Offer (pilot):** For 30 days, using the plant's own forms and rules, the founder:
1. Texts and calls customers whose cut sheets are missing ahead of cut day and sends them the plant's form link. The founder never takes cutting instructions verbally.
2. Chases overdue pickups and sends the plant's payment link.
3. Sends the plant a daily status sheet.

Price: $500 for 30 days. Ask for 50% up front and the rest at day 30 if missing sheets and overdue pickups drop by 50% or more against the week-0 baseline.

## 9. Cheapest test and kill criterion

- **Test:** 60 dials plus the interview, costing $60 or less and about 40 founder-hours over 8 working days.
- **Kill if any of these is true by day 10:**
  1. Fewer than **10** decision-maker conversations (owner or GM) from 60 dials.
  2. Fewer than **2** plants agree to a paid pilot at **$300 or more with a deposit by day 14**. Verbal interest does not count.
  3. **60% or more** of reached decision-makers say missing cut sheets or overdue pickups cost them under 2 staff-hours a week, or that their current form or software already handles it.
- **Post-pilot kill:** Missing sheets plus overdue pickups fall less than 50% from baseline, **or** neither pilot renews at $300/month or more.
- **Continue only if:** 2 or more deposits and 1 or more renewals. Then test a $750 price and a lighter software-assisted version.

## 10. Strongest contrary evidence

1. **Commodity tooling already exists and is in use.** Processors already publish free Google Forms and Jotform cut sheets (for example, [Hudson Meats Jotform](https://form.jotform.com/213432939407155), seen in search results, not opened). The first prospect already has online forms.
2. **Purpose-built SaaS is entering now.** Cleaver launched nationwide on June 24, 2026, for independent processors. It captures cut sheets, handles traceability and HACCP logs, and offers a 14-day free trial ([National Provisioner](https://www.provisioneronline.com/articles/120813-cleaver-all-in-one-software-built-for-independent-meat-processors), verified). SmartLocker (online cut sheets, pickup reminders) and Meat Order Pro exist per search results (**unverified**, G2 returned 403).
3. **Plants push the pain onto producers.** "Default cut if no sheet" policies mean the processor may not feel enough pain to pay.
4. **The plant's real bottleneck may be labor and kill capacity, not admin.** This comes from general industry knowledge and is **unverified**. If interviews confirm it, A1 is a vitamin, not a painkiller.
5. **Low ceiling.** About $5.5k/month contribution at full utilization. That is a job, not equity.

**What would overturn the lean-no-go:** 2 or more deposits from the test, **and** plants reporting 5 or more staff-hours a week, or $1,000 or more a month in tied-up freezer space or receivables.

## 11. Alternatives considered

- **Do nothing / keep the $1,000:** A valid choice. Loses about $0 and keeps the cash for a better-evidenced idea.
- **Cheaper option, $0:** Take paid hourly front-office or remote admin work for one processor or farm. This gives you the same learning with zero sales risk, and the ~$13 to $23 per hour above is the bar to beat.
- **B2 (GAP plans):** Revisit only if a 2026 FSCSC round is confirmed on farmers.gov. That would subsidize the farm's fee.
- **B1 (FSMA 204):** Revisit in 2027 as July 2028 nears.

## 13. Buyer interview (owner/GM of a very small processor)

1. "Think about the last animal that hit its cut day with no cut sheet on file. What did your office do, how many calls or texts did it take, and did it end in a default cut or a delay?"
2. "In the last 30 days, about how many cut sheets came in late, incomplete, or needed a callback to fix? How do you know that number: paper, a Google or Jotform form, or software?"
3. "Right now, how many finished orders are in your freezer past the pickup date, and how old is the oldest? What does that cost you in freezer space or in processing fees you haven't collected?"
4. "Who handles booking calls, 'is my meat ready' calls, and pickup reminders in your busy season, and how many hours a week does that take from them?"
5. "What have you already tried for this, like online forms, Cleaver, SmartLocker or other software, an office hire, or stricter deadlines? What did it cost, and why did you keep it or drop it?"
6. "If someone outside your shop chased missing cut sheets and overdue pickups for 30 days, using your forms and your rules, and the second half of the fee was due only if both dropped by half, what would you pay for that month? Who besides you would need to approve it?"

## 14. Gating risks

- **Access:** Owners are on the cutting floor, and fall is peak season. Gate: 10 or more decision-maker conversations.
- **Trust and data:** You need written authorization to contact the plant's customers and must handle their contact data carefully.
- **Messaging law:** Texting customers raises TCPA and consent questions. **Unverified, not legal advice.** Use only numbers the plant has consent for, send transactional messages only, and have a lawyer or the plant confirm before scaling.
- **Liability:** A wrong cut caused by misheard instructions. Mitigation: never take instructions verbally; send customers to the plant's own form.
- **Substitution:** A plant adopts Cleaver or another tool mid-pilot.
- **Price ceiling:** Very small plants are, by definition, under $2.5M in sales or under 10 employees.
- **Founder time:** Delivery competes with the 60 h/week. The model assumes 20 h per pilot.
