## Decision

**Run a $46 test this week on one idea: a walking route cleaning storefront glass in Northeast Minneapolis (Central Ave NE / East Hennepin). Don't commit to the business yet.**

Confidence: low. No candidate clears the bar on current evidence. The glass route is the only one that could, and only if at least ~6% of owners and managers you pitch actually sign up. Nobody has measured that, so the test measures it.

**The bar I applied:**
1. Every legal and insurance requirement can be met with $1,000 or less.
2. No vehicle needed.
3. Conservative 90-day cash is at least $0.
4. Realistic path to $3,000/mo by month 6 at $40+ per delivery hour.
5. Demand can be tested for $100 or less within 2 weeks.

**Why B dies.** The demand driver is real and verified:
- Commercial properties must clear sidewalks within 4 daytime hours after snow stops ([city page, 10/22/2026](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/getting-around/snow/snow-clearing/sidewalk-snow-clearing)).
- If the city clears it, a 40 ft commercial frontage costs the owner $99.20 × 1.5 + $25 = **$173.80 per violation** ([city fee sheet, 2/2026](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/media/-www-content-assets/documents/Sidewalk-snow-clearing.pdf)).

But this winter's weather kills it:
- NOAA's 11/19/2026 outlook favors above-normal Dec-Feb temperatures for the Upper Mississippi Valley, with a strong El Niño as the main driver ([CPC](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/predictions/long_range/fxus05.html)).
- State climatologists say El Niño winters have averaged 24% less Twin Cities snow ([MN DNR](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/climate/journal/el-nino-2026.html)).
- On top of that, the city already lists 9 shoveling providers ([list revised 10/21/2026](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/media/-www-content-assets/documents/Sidewalk-Snow-Shoveling-Services-2025-26-Oct.pdf)), and the business is seasonal.

Revisit it for fall 2027: the same NOAA discussion shows a weak-to-moderate La Niña for 2027-28, which usually means more snow, and glass customers would be a ready list to sell it to.

**Why C dies.** Location gives you no advantage, so you compete with every national firm. Mistakes flow straight into clients' tax filings, and nothing in your stated background shows bookkeeping skill. That last point is my judgment, not sourced.

**Screened out before the shortlist:**
- Collecting receivables for clients: needs a $500 state collection agency license, and operating without one is a misdemeanor ([MN statute 332.33](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2025/2025-10-19%2013:07:26+00:00/cite/332.33/pdf), from a search snippet, not opened).
- Kitchen hood cleaning: city permit, plus you'd need a vehicle and equipment.
- Office janitorial: modeled at about $27 per delivery hour once bus time to each site is counted. It's the fallback if A fails.

## First-90-day economics (run in code, Nov 30 to Feb 28)

**Assumptions:**
- Price: $25 per visit, every two weeks (low end of an unverified $25-100 range).
- 20 owner/manager conversations per pitch day: 3 pitch days/wk for 4 weeks, then 2/wk.
- 25% of winter visits lost to cold.
- 20 minutes per stop.
- About 1% of accounts cancel per week.
- Payments arrive 2 weeks after the visit.
- Setup costs $350: kit ~$180, assumed-name filing ~$50, printing ~$40, first month of insurance ~$60, ~$20 winter additive (cold-weather cleaning solution).

| Case (share of pitches that sign) | Accounts by day 90 | Gross billed | Cash in | Costs | Net | Hours | Net $/hr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (1.5%) | 8 | $532 | $390 | $705 | **-$315** | 169 | -$1.86 |
| Conservative (3%) | 17 | $1,063 | $780 | $721 | **+$59** | 176 | $0.33 |
| Base (6%) | 33 | $2,126 | $1,559 | $753 | **+$807** | 190 | $4.24 |

- **Sensitivity:** in the 3% case, dropping the price to $20 makes net -$91, and losing 50% of winter visits makes it -$190.
- **Month 6:** at 3% the route bills about $1,560/mo; at 6%, $3,119/mo. **The share of pitches that sign is the assumption that decides go or no-go.**
- **Gross while cleaning:** about $75 per hour. Your money is made or lost in the selling.
- **Worst case:** peak cash at risk stays under ~$750 in every case.

## Strongest contrary evidence

1. **You'd launch in the off-season.** Window cleaners report rescheduling below about 15°F ([trade forum](https://community.windowcleaner.com/t/winter-weather-cleaning/45754), anecdotal). Even the base case nets about $4/hr for 90 days.
2. **The market already has a serious competitor.** Fish Window Cleaning has multiple Minneapolis franchise territories doing exactly this storefront route work ([Fish Window Cleaning Minneapolis](https://www.fishwindowcleaning.com/Minneapolis), not opened).
3. **Each account is tiny.** At about $54/mo per store, $3k/mo takes about 55 paying stores. That likely exceeds one neighborhood cluster, so you'd have to add Lake St and Nicollet.

## Demand: confirmed vs hypothesized

- **Confirmed:**
  - The legal requirement and fee for sidewalk clearing (B).
  - The city's 311 data, which I counted: about 5,600 sidewalk snow/ice cases in 2025 and about 4,200 in 2026 so far. That shows people not complying, not people willing to pay.
  - The franchise competitor suggests storefronts do pay for glass cleaning, but that's indirect.
- **Hypothesized:** everything about these 5 prospects, the share of pitches that sign, winter willingness to buy, and the price.
- **Confirmed demand from any named business: zero.**

## The test (about $46, Dec 1-5)

**Before Dec 1:**
1. Register as a Minneapolis solicitor (free).
2. Get 2 online liability insurance quotes (free).

**Offer:** "$25 per visit every 2 weeks, front glass and entry door inside and out. First visit $10. Cancel anytime. Starts the week of Dec 14." Close means a signed one-page agreement plus a card on file or a $10 deposit, refundable if you don't start.

**Cost:**
- Printing: ~$20 (estimate)
- 4 day passes: $16 (verified)
- Clipboard: ~$10

Don't buy the cleaning kit until the test passes.

**Rule, after 40 conversations with an owner or manager:**
- **Pass:** 3 or more closes (7.5%+), and an insurance quote of $75/mo or less. Buy the kit and start.
- **Inconclusive:** exactly 2 closes. Run 40 more on Lake St Dec 8-12; pass if you reach 5 or more out of 80.
- **Fail:** 0 or 1 close, or insurance over $100/mo or declined. Kill it and spend nothing more.

Forty conversations is a small sample, so treat a pass as "keep going," not proof.

**What would flip the decision:** a fail, or more than half of the 40 already having a vendor at $25 or less.
