## 1. Why no compliant profile exists

I opened Google's live policy pages today, November 23, 2026. None of them shows an effective date, so "in force today" means "live as of today."

| Gate | Google's rule (verified) | This business | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic eligibility | "If your business either has a physical location that customers can visit, or travels to customers where they are, you can create a Business Profile" ([3038177](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)) | Neither: no visits either way | **Fail** |
| Storefront at the $59 address | "If your business rents a physical mailing address but doesn't operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile" (same page) | Mail-only address | **Fail** |
| Storefront at home | The location must be "staffed by your team and able to receive customers during its stated hours" (same page) | No customers visit | **Fail** |
| Service-area business | Defined as one that "visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn't serve customers at their business address" ([9157481](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en)) | Delivers files remotely, never in person | **Fail** |
| Virtual office for a service-area business | "Service-area businesses can't list a 'virtual' office unless that office is staffed during business hours" ([3038177](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)) | Not staffed | **Fail** |

None of the pages says outright that online-only businesses are banned. That conclusion is my inference, but it rests on the eligibility sentence, which allows only those two models. The vendor's line that the address "can be listed on Google Maps" goes against Google's own wording. Being able to type an address into Maps doesn't make it eligible. I'm not quoting a suspension clause because I didn't open one, so treat "the profile risks suspension" as unverified.

## 2. Permitted setups vs. material changes

**Permitted under your stated constraints: none.**

Each of these changes would make you eligible, but each one breaks a constraint you set:

1. **Go on-site.** Do data cleanup at clients' offices and list as a service-area business with your home address hidden. This breaks "never visit customers."
2. **Receive clients at home during posted hours.** This breaks "no customer visits" and makes your home address public.
3. **Rent a real staffed office.** A coworking office qualifies only with "clear signage," customers received there during business hours, and your staff present. This breaks the constraints and costs far more than $59.

Faking any of these (fake hours, fake travel) is misrepresentation, not a configuration.

**CAN-SPAM matters here.** Every commercial email needs "your valid physical postal address." That can be a USPS-registered P.O. box or a private mailbox at a registered mail-receiving agency ([FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), verified; dated Aug 2023, edited Jan 2024). You must honor opt-outs within 10 business days, and penalties run up to $53,088 per email (2024 figure; may since have been adjusted for inflation). So the mailbox has one legitimate use: the footer of your outreach emails. A USPS P.O. box probably beats $59/month for that, but local prices are unverified, so check usps.com.

**Upwork backup (unverified):** Connects reportedly cost $0.15 each, the Basic plan gets about 10 free a month, and the freelancer fee is a variable 0–15% ([Upwork: Is Upwork free](https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free), [Connects help article](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062898-Understanding-and-using-Connects)). Check these in your account before you spend anything.
