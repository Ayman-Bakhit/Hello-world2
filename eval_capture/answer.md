## The two pitches: both misleading

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Registration costs only $300" | **Half-true.** $300 is the FMCSA application fee for broker authority. But FMCSA "will not register a broker until a surety bond or trust fund" of $75,000 is in effect. | Verified: [49 CFR 360.3T(f)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-360.3T) (the original §360.3 is suspended; the "T" version is the one in force); [49 CFR 387.307(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-387.307) |
| "Every broker must deposit $75,000 cash" | **False as stated.** The rule is a $75,000 surety bond (Form BMC-84) **or** a trust fund (BMC-85). Only the trust-fund route needs $75k of assets, and those are limited to cash, irrevocable letters of credit from a federally insured bank, or Treasury bonds. | Verified: [387.307(a)–(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-387.307) |

eCFR text read as of its Nov 19, 2026 point-in-time, the latest available.

## Requirements in effect on Nov 23, 2026

1. **Authority.** Apply for property broker registration and pay the $300 fee ([360.3T](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-360.3T)). The fee is non-refundable once FMCSA accepts the filing. *Verified.*
2. **Financial responsibility.** You need a $75,000 BMC-84 bond or BMC-85 trust fund in effect continuously. It can only be cancelled with 30 days' notice. *Verified:* [387.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-387.307).
3. **Process agent.** You file Form BOC-3, covering every state where you have an office or write contracts ([366.2T](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-366.2T), [366.4T](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-366.4T)). *Verified.*
   - Blanket-agent companies typically file it. Their price is *unverified*, so it's a variable in the math below.
4. **Deadlines.**
   - FMCSA publishes a summary of your application in the FMCSA Register.
   - The bond and BOC-3 are due within **20 days** of that publication ([365.109T](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-365.109T)).
   - Anyone can protest your application for **10 days** after publication. If no one does, the grant becomes effective ([365.115](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-365.115)). *Verified.*
5. **UCR registration.** Brokers pay the smallest-bracket fee: **$46 for 2025–2026 and $55 for 2027** ([91 FR 56063, Sep 1, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/01/2026-17893/fees-for-the-unified-carrier-registration-plan-and-agreement)). *Verified.* Whether a December start owes 2026, 2027 or both is *unverified*, so the math budgets both.
6. **Records.** For each load, keep the shipper, the carrier and its registration number, the bill of lading, your compensation, and the date you paid the carrier. Keep them 3 years ([371.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-371.3)). *Verified.*
7. **No pre-authority selling.** A "broker" is anyone who "arranges, **or offers to arrange**" transportation for compensation ([371.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-371.2)). Quoting the customer as a broker before your authority is active already counts.
   - Penalty: up to **$13,676 per violation** for knowingly brokering without registration or the bond ([Part 386 App. B (g)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-386/appendix-Appendix%20B%20to%20Part%20386)). *Verified.*

## Changes in 2026

- **Jan 16, 2026: the bond rule tightened.** This is the "Broker and Freight Forwarder Financial Responsibility" rule ([387.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-387.307)). *Verified.*
  - Trust-fund assets must be convertible to cash within 7 days.
  - If a paid claim drops your bond below $75k, the surety must notify FMCSA within 2 business days.
  - FMCSA then suspends your authority within 7 business days unless you cure it.
  - Practical effect: one unpaid carrier claim can shut you down fast.
- **Jul 21, 2026: typo fix only** to 387.307(e) cross-references. No substantive change ([91 FR 45653](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/21/2026-14701/general-technical-organizational-conforming-and-correcting-amendments-to-the-federal-motor-carrier)). *Verified.*
- **Motus replaces URS as the registration system** ([91 FR 23144, Apr 29, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/29/2026-08334/availability-of-motus-fmcsas-new-registration-system)). *Verified.*
  - It requires **identity proofing**: a smartphone, a government ID and a face scan.
  - It adds business verification.
  - Paper forms get "a minimum timeframe of **eight business days**" for initial review, plus extra time if the application is flagged for vetting.
  - MC number elimination and BOC-3 process changes were **postponed**.
  - **Unverified:** whether Phase II, originally planned for Q2 2026, is actually live. FMCSA's website blocked every automated fetch I tried.
- **Sep 1, 2026:** the UCR fee for 2027 rises to $55 (see item 5 above).

Even in the middle row, the load has to tender on Dec 15–23. And the bond has to be filed by then, which only works if the surety files it within days.

## Other gates that could kill it

- **Cash float.** After funding you'll have about $0. If the carrier expects payment before the shipper pays you, you can't cover it. An unpaid carrier claim then hits the bond and triggers a fast suspension. You need written terms where the shipper pays first, or factoring. Factoring cost is *unverified*.
- **Demand.** "Could offer a load" is not a commitment. Get the lane, rate, date and payment terms in writing.
- **Insurance.** Federal rules require only the bond. Shippers often demand contingent cargo insurance. That's *unverified*, but if this shipper requires it, it's a cost you haven't budgeted.

## Alternatives

1. **Freight agent under a licensed broker** (the best fit for this load). You bring the customer, and the broker's authority, bond and cash float cover the load for a commission split. Cost is about $0 and it can happen within days.
   - *Unverified:* the exact legal structure. Confirm that the broker, not you, is the contracting party.
2. **Get your own authority first, with no load deadline.** File once the bond is quoted, and take the customer's loads after the authority is active.
3. **Do nothing.** You lose $0, but you also lose the customer relationship.

**What would flip this to GO:**
- A surety quotes ≤ about $550 total with no collateral and files in ≤ 3 business days.
- Your application publishes within about 5 business days.
- The shipper agrees in writing to pay before the carrier is due.

**What would make it a firm NO-GO:**
- Any collateral requirement, or a premium above about $650.
- A vetting flag on your application.
- The customer won't commit to a date or payment terms.
