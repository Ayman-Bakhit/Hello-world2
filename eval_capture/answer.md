**Verdict: inconclusive. No idea is a confirmed go.** One is worth testing for $100: a done-for-you energy benchmarking compliance service for owners of 50,000-99,999 sq ft buildings. I'm fairly confident in the ranking, but not that anyone will actually pay for this.

**The three candidates**
- **Sidewalk snow-clearing route on foot: rejected as a business.** Minneapolis gives commercial properties 4 daytime hours to clear sidewalks after snow stops, and the city clears it and bills the owner if they don't ([city rules](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/getting-around/snow/snow-clearing/sidewalk-snow-clearing)). The demand driver is real. But it only runs Nov-Mar, one person can cover about 12-15 storefronts inside the 4-hour window, and slip-and-fall insurance costs about $45-132/month. It's fine winter side cash, not a business.
- **Monthly bookkeeping: rejected.** Nothing forces a buyer to act, and you'd have no edge or track record to win trust.
- **Benchmarking compliance: conditional pick.** Minn. Stat. 216C.331 says metro buildings of 50k+ sq ft must report energy use every June 1, with a possible $1,000 fine. The law lets an authorized agent file for the owner, and no license is required ([statute](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/216C.331)). The free state tool is ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. It's all laptop work, costs nearly nothing to deliver, and Minneapolis publishes the list of buildings that must comply.

**Brutal part**
- **A regular job wins the first 90 days.** Conservative 90-day net is **+$640** for benchmarking (worst case -$150) and $720-980 for snow. A W-2 job at $16-20/h for 50 h/week is about $10-13k over the same 90 days (assumed wage, not verified).
- **The biggest argument against benchmarking:** the state pays a contractor to run a **free help desk** for owners ([Commerce page](https://mn.gov/commerce/energy/industry-government/benchmarking/building-owners.jsp)). Saint Paul got 93% of buildings to comply on their own. And I found **zero evidence that anyone in Minnesota pays a third party for this.**
- **What's confirmed:** the legal obligation, the deadlines and the fine.
- **What's only a guess:** that owners will pay $395 per building, and how many buildings are behind.
