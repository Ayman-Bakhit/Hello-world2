# PRODUCT_DECISIONS: the canonical product decision record

This is the authoritative record of the product and economics decisions a launch depends on. It records what is **decided**, what is **pending**, what is only an **engineering default awaiting approval**, and what each open decision blocks. It is a decision-resolution document, not an execution one: real execution stays disabled, and nothing here signs, sends, mints, creates liquidity, routes fees or moves money.

Rules this record follows:
- Nothing is decided here that the product owner has not decided or the existing requirements do not support. Where neither applies the decision is **PENDING** and the exact decision needed is stated.
- No address, pool, mint, metadata URI, signature or observation appears anywhere in it (the only addresses in the code policy are public program ids).
- "Enforced", "immutable" and "on-chain" are not used for anything that is not.
- Code mirrors this record: `packages/shared/src/deploymentPolicy.ts` (`DEPLOYMENT_POLICY`, version 3, 21 decisions) and the readiness evaluator in `readiness.ts`. A test fails if the status table below disagrees with the code or omits a decision. Approvals are recorded only by a reviewed code change (approver, date, reference, version); no API, query or client field can approve anything.
- **Approval is not implementation.** An approved decision says what the product wants. It does not mean any mechanism exists, was audited, or is mainnet-ready, and readiness keeps blocking until the mechanism does.

### Intended, minted and permanently unissued supply
These are three different numbers. Only the second exists on-chain.

| Quantity | Meaning | Example (intended 100,000,000) |
|---|---|---|
| Intended supply | The total the launch is defined around (`totalSupply` in the configuration). **Not** an amount that exists on-chain. | 100,000,000 |
| Minted supply | The sum of the issued roles, created on-chain by minting: creator + liquidity (48%). The only supply Token Proof expects to observe. | 8,000,000 creator + 40,000,000 liquidity = 48,000,000 |
| Permanently unissued | The 52% role. Never minted, never held by anyone, **not burned**, and never implied to exist on-chain. | 52,000,000 |

Never read the intended supply as circulating or on-chain: with intended 100,000,000 the chain shows a mint supply of 48,000,000, not 100,000,000. The alternative of minting 100% and burning 52% was rejected because it would put a different, larger supply on-chain first and make the reduction depend on a burn nobody has specified.

"Permanent" has a hard Solana precondition. The unissued share is permanent only once the mint authority is revoked (the plan's `revoke-mint-authority` instruction, which is a plan, not an event). A launch that keeps the mint authority is refused (`UNISSUED_SUPPLY_NOT_PERMANENT`), and readiness has the gate `UNISSUED_SUPPLY_PERMANENT`. Until the revoke is observed on-chain, nothing here claims the supply is permanently reduced.

## Where the existing requirements conflict or are ambiguous
These are surfaced, not silently resolved. Nothing in the code was changed to hide them.
1. **"Creator allocation" means two things.** `creatorAllocationPercent` is a share of token SUPPLY; the 60% creator bucket of the fee split is a share of FEES, and the launch UI labels that bucket "Configured creator allocation" (`launchModel.ts`). Still open as a naming problem.
2. **The 8/40/52 allocation is fixed (resolved in pass 2).** Creator 8%, liquidity 40%, permanently unissued 52%. The creator and liquidity fields in the launch configuration still exist as schema fields, but any launch whose values differ is refused (`ALLOCATION_MODEL_MISMATCH`). Remaining cleanup: the launch wizard should show them as fixed.
3. **"Burn" was ambiguous (resolved in pass 2).** The owner clarified: the 52% is never minted and not burned (`PERMANENTLY_UNISSUED`). It is permanent only once the mint authority is revoked.
4. **The launch tax reserve destination: the configuration and the owner differ.** The launch configuration stores a creator-controlled address for the tax reserve (`destinationType: creator_controlled`). The owner says the destination and custody model are still PENDING. The configuration field is unchanged but is now explicitly a compatibility field: it is shown as PENDING, blocks readiness, and is not treated as an approved destination or final custody (pass 2).
5. **A custom program and the approved defaults.** A custom Solana program is the chosen enforcement direction while SPL Token classic is approved. This is compatible (a program can route fees without a Token-2022 extension), but the program is not designed, so whether classic SPL Token is sufficient is unproven until it is. If the design needs a Token-2022 extension, that is a new decision that would invalidate the token program approval.
6. **The Slice 3 demo tokens use varying splits** (for example 7000/500/1500/1000 and 5000/1000/2500/1500) over a generic `lifetimeFeesCents`, while the launch accepts only 6000/1500/1500/1000. The owner has now said the split divides actual fees from the defined mechanism, which matches "fees", but the demo's per-token varying splits are inconsistent with a fixed split and were not changed.
7. **Metadata is mutable by default.** `updateAuthority` defaults to `creator`; creator updates are PENDING while the owner requires that a change must not silently remain verified.
8. **Charity verification is fixture or admin data.** "Verified" does not yet mean verified in the real world; the owner requires the payout wallet to be independently verifiable before verified charity routing is claimed.
9. **The owner form's name field was a placeholder.** Approvals are recorded against the role. `SUPPLY_SEMANTICS` was approved in pass 2.
10. **Stale docs** (`DEPLOYMENT.md`, `THREAT_MODEL.md`) were refreshed and now point here.

## 1. Initial token supply and allocation model
**Decided and approved (owner, pass 2, `SUPPLY_SEMANTICS`):** a fixed total supply, divided in basis points, where every unit has exactly one explicit role, there is no silent remainder, a permanent supply reduction must be explicit, and the product configuration must match the canonical allocation.

**Decided and approved (owner, pass 2, `SUPPLY_ALLOCATION_MODEL` v2):** creator 8% (800 bps), liquidity 40% (4000), permanently unissued 52% (5200), charity 0%, tax reserve 0%, protocol 0%: 10000 bps. The model is fixed and not configurable; there is no tokenomics editor. Charity, tax reserve and protocol receive no initial token supply; their shares exist only as fee allocations. The 52% is the explicit role `PERMANENTLY_UNISSUED`: never minted, not burned. The deployment plan carries exactly these roles with exact base-unit amounts (a share that would need rounding is refused) and reports intended, minted and unissued supply separately. Token Proof expects the minted supply (48%).

**Not decided:**
- `ALLOCATION_LOCKS_AND_VESTING` (PENDING): the creator's 8% is fixed; whether it is transferable immediately, locked or vested is PENDING, and immediate transferability must not be assumed. LP locking is part of the liquidity decision.

Initial token supply allocation and the fee split remain different economic concepts.

## 2. What the 60/15/15/10 split divides
**Decided (owner, `FEE_SPLIT` and `FEE_SPLIT_SCOPE`, approved):** the split divides **actual fees generated by the defined fee mechanism**, whenever that mechanism generates a fee. It does not divide trading volume, token supply, market cap or gross launch volume. Creator 60%, tax reserve 15%, charity 15%, protocol 10% (6000/1500/1500/1000 bps). A 1,000 USD actual fee routes 600 to the creator, 150 to the tax reserve, 150 to the charity and 100 to the protocol.

**Still open:** the exact fee source and venue. The owner states they must be specified as part of the liquidity and fee routing decisions; until then there is no defined fee mechanism to generate a fee, who pays it, or whether it is per transaction. The split is not enforced by anything today.

## 3. Fee routing and enforcement mechanism (`FEE_ROUTING_MECHANISM`)
**Decided (owner choice, approved): D, a custom Solana program,** and the split must be objectively verifiable from on-chain state and transactions. The split is **not** considered implemented because a backend calculates or routes 60/15/15/10. A custom program must enforce the routing rules, subject to security and smart-contract review before mainnet.

**This is a choice, not an implementation.** No program exists, none is designed, specified, audited or deployed, and there is no program id. The decision record says `implemented: false` and `enforcesSplit: false`, so `FEE_ROUTING_ENFORCEMENT_NOT_IMPLEMENTED` keeps `FEE_ROUTING_ENFORCEABLE` blocked. Choosing D also makes the smart-contract review applicable (it is PENDING, reviewer TBD) and ties the design to the liquidity venue (the program must receive the fees the venue generates, which is unknown).

For reference, the options that were evaluated:

Evaluated options. "Verifiable" means an observer could objectively confirm the 60/15/15/10 outcome from chain data.

| | A. Application-level routing | B. Venue or router support | C. On-chain fee vault | D. Custom program |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Can enforce | Nothing on-chain: the platform chooses to forward funds | Whatever the venue's fee settings allow; typically one fee recipient or a venue-defined split | Splitting funds deposited into the vault, if the vault program is the only way out | Any rule the program encodes, including the split and authority limits |
| Cannot enforce | That the platform actually forwards, or forwards the right amounts | A split the venue does not support; changes the venue's admin can make | That fees reach the vault in the first place, unless the venue pays into it | Anything outside the program's own accounts |
| Custody | Platform or a platform-controlled wallet touches funds: conflicts with the non-custodial rule for user assets | Venue and fee recipient hold funds; platform custody only if it is the recipient | Funds sit in program-controlled accounts | Funds sit in program-controlled accounts |
| Upgrade or admin risk | The platform can change behavior at will | The venue's admin and upgrade authority | Program upgrade authority and any vault admin | Program upgrade authority; the largest surface |
| Works with SPL Token | Yes | Yes, venue permitting | Yes, with a program | Yes, with a program |
| Needs a custom program | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Needs an external venue | Optional | Yes | Optional | Optional |
| Audit burden | Low code, but no on-chain guarantee to audit | Review of the integration; trust in the venue | Program audit | Program audit plus governance review |
| User signing | Minimal | The creator signs setup at launch | Creator and users sign vault interactions | Same, per program design |
| 60/15/15/10 objectively verifiable | No | Only if the venue's configuration and payouts are observable and match | Yes, if routing is complete and observable | Yes, by design |

Consequences of option D: the program needs a design that says where fees come from, an upgrade-authority policy (an upgradeable program can change the rule, which weakens "verifiable"), custody of fees in program-controlled accounts, user signing steps, and an audit before mainnet. Whether the chosen liquidity venue can pay fees to a program is unknown, and decisions should be taken in this order: liquidity venue, then the program design.

## 6. Metadata hosting (PENDING hosting, DECIDED document format)
Decided (approved, `METADATA_DOCUMENT` v2): a Metaplex-style JSON document (name, symbol, description, image if set, external_url if set, `seller_fee_basis_points: 0`), canonicalized with sorted keys and compact UTF-8, with its **sha256 recorded in the plan and the expected state**. ## 7. Charity model
**Decided (owner, `CHARITY_PAYOUT_MODEL` v2, approved):** the charity receives **no** initial token supply and **15% of actual fees**. One verified payout wallet per charity: the charity must be VERIFIED; exactly one verified, well-formed wallet must exist; it is part of the plan and expected state; a change to it makes the recorded plan stale. The payout wallet must be independently verifiable before the launch can claim verified charity routing. No donation is executed and no charity funds touch platform custody today (nothing is routed at all).

**Pending (`CHARITY_VERIFICATION_GOVERNANCE`):** who verifies a charity and who controls verification; the evidence for a real (non-fixture) verification; what happens when a wallet changes or a charity is suspended after launch; whether charity funds ever touch platform custody; and how payouts are independently verified. Fixture charities are separate from real verified charities and cannot satisfy a real launch.

## 8. Tax reserve model
**Decided (owner, `TAX_RESERVE_MODEL` v2, approved):** the launch tax reserve is a **fee/revenue allocation**: a designated recipient of 15% of the actual fee stream defined in decision 2. It is not an initial token allocation, not the creator's personal Tax Reserve (Slice 10) and not a USDC account that is funded before any fee exists. It shares none of the personal Tax Reserve's tables.

**Pending (`TAX_RESERVE_FUNDING`):** the exact destination and custody model: whether it is a wallet or program-controlled, who controls and can change it, withdrawal rules, how it is reconciled and shown in Token Proof, and what happens when it is unavailable. The existing configuration field stores a creator-controlled address; see the conflict noted above. Nothing funds it and no money movement is implemented.
