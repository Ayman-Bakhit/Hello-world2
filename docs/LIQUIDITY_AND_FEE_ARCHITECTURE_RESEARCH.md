## 1. Executive summary

The question: where does the fee originate, and how can a custom program enforce 60/15/15/10 on-chain?

1. **In every existing AMM read here, the fee originates inside the venue's own swap instruction.** It is deducted from the trader's input, computed from fee parameters the venue controls, and credited to the venue's own accounting (LP reserves, per-position fee counters, a protocol bucket, in Raydium CP-swap also a fund bucket and a creator bucket). Our program never sees the swap [SRC]. So our program can only do two things: own one of those buckets, and split what it withdraws from it.
2. **A custom program can enforce the split of whatever reaches it.** It cannot enforce the total fee rate, the venue's own take, whether the pool is paused, or what traders do on other pools. Those belong to the venue.
3. **The split cannot be a token-wide tax with SPL Token classic.** Anyone can create other pools for the same mint on several venues without permission [SRC], and classic SPL Token has no hook on transfers. The only enforceable scope is "fees generated by the defined launch pool(s)". That matches the owner's wording ("actual fees generated by the defined fee mechanism") and must be the wording everywhere.
4. **A wrapper or router (Architecture D) enforces nothing.** A trader can call the pool directly or through an aggregator and never touch it.
5. **Two real options enforce the split:**
   - **Architecture A:** an existing position-based AMM, where our immutable splitter program owns the liquidity position (so the program, not a person, can collect its fees) and the position is permanently locked. Orca Whirlpool and Meteora DAMM v2 both have the needed mechanics [SRC]. The split is enforced; the total fee and the venue's take stay under the venue admin's control [SRC].
   - **Architecture C:** our own pool program, where our swap instruction takes the fee. Nobody else can change the fee, but it needs a security-critical AMM, an audit, and aggregator integration to get volume.
6. **Recommended for the owner to consider: A.** Runner-up: C. If the owner needs "nobody but our immutable code can change the fee rate or pause trading", only C satisfies that. If the owner accepts a disclosed venue-admin trust, A is smaller and easier to audit. This choice cannot be made from technical evidence alone.
7. **Raydium CP-swap is a poor fit.** Its creator-fee feature is not available to a permissionless pool (it needs an admin-issued permission) [SRC], the admin can set the protocol's share of the creator fee up to 100% [SRC], and the admin can switch off swaps and withdrawals per pool [SRC].
8. **A correction to how percentages read on-chain.** Only 48% of the intended supply is ever minted. Measured against what exists on-chain, the creator holds 8/48 = 16.67% and the pool initially holds 40/48 = 83.33%. Labels such as "creator 8%" are of the intended supply. The UI and Token Proof must show both bases.
9. **Token Proof needs new checks** (program IDs, upgrade authority, the program's immutable recipient table, position ownership, lock state, venue fee parameters, pool status). The current observation model cannot express them (section 9). None are implemented.
10. **Token-2022 would not rescue the design.** A transfer hook could in principle tax every transfer. Raydium CP-swap rejects mints with a transfer hook by default [SRC]. Not a change to the approved decision.

## 3. Fee model

### 3.1 The concepts that must not be mixed

| Concept | What it is | Where it lives | Governed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Token supply allocation | How the intended supply is divided into roles: 8% creator, 40% liquidity, 52% permanently unissued | Mint supply, token accounts | `SUPPLY_ALLOCATION_MODEL`. Fixed. |
| Launch allocation | The same thing as supply allocation in this product. Not a fee. | n/a | n/a |
| Trading fee (total) | The percentage the venue takes from each swap input | The venue's swap instruction | The venue's parameters. Changeable by the venue admin in the venues read [SRC]. |
| LP fee | The part of the trading fee that belongs to liquidity providers | Pool reserves or per-position counters | Whoever owns the LP position or LP tokens |
| Protocol/venue fee | The part the venue keeps | A venue-owned bucket | Venue admin. Not ours. |
| Creator fee | A venue feature that credits a "pool creator" bucket. Exists in Raydium CP-swap and Meteora DBC. | Pool state | Venue config |
| Creator revenue | What the launch's creator ends up receiving: 60% of the fee amount that reaches our program | Creator's destination | Our splitter program |
| Tax reserve allocation | 15% of the same fee amount. A fee/revenue allocation, never a token allocation. Destination PENDING. | Tax reserve destination | Our splitter program |
| Charity allocation | 15% of the same fee amount. Never a token allocation. | Charity destination | Our splitter program |
| Protocol allocation | 10% of the same fee amount | Protocol destination (PENDING) | Our splitter program |

**The 8/40/52 supply model and the 60/15/15/10 fee model are independent.** The first decides who holds tokens at launch. The second decides how fee revenue is divided after trading starts. Nothing in one derives from the other. The pool holds 40% of intended supply as the liquidity role; it does not "own" any fee allocation, and charity, tax reserve and protocol hold no tokens at launch.

### 3.2 The denominator problem

"60/15/15/10 of the fees" is defined only relative to a base. The base is **the fee amount that reaches our program**, not the total fee a trader pays. Under Architecture A the base excludes the venue's own take and excludes the fees earned by anyone else's liquidity in the same pool. Under Architecture C the base is the full fee our swap charges. The product copy must name the base.

### 3.3 Rounding
Integer division leaves dust. A deterministic rule is needed (for example: floor three shares, the remainder goes to one named recipient). This is a small product decision (section 14).

### 4.2 Detail by question (A to P)

Common to the position-based venues (Orca, DAMM v2; DLMM by IDL; CLMM partly):

- **A. Who owns/controls the pool?** The pool account is created by the venue program. Nobody owns a pool. The venue's config authorities can change its parameters. The pool's reserves sit in vaults controlled by the venue program's authority PDA [SRC].
- **B. Who controls swap execution?** Anyone can call swap. There is no per-pool allow list in Orca, DAMM v2 or CP-swap. [SRC, inferred from the absence of a signer check on the swap accounts.] DLMM has `set_pre_activation_swap_address`, which restricts swaps only before activation [IDL].
- **C. Where are trading fees calculated?** In the swap instruction, from the pool's fee rate (Orca: `swap_manager.rs`; CP-swap: `curve/fees.rs`; DAMM v2: `state/fee.rs` and the swap handlers) [SRC].
- **D. Where are fees collected?** Orca: per-position `fee_owed_a/b`, plus a protocol bucket in the pool. DAMM v2: per-position counters, plus `protocol_a_fee/protocol_b_fee` in the pool. CP-swap: inside the vault balances; separate counters for protocol, fund and creator. [SRC]
- **E. Who receives them?** Position fees go to whoever holds the position NFT and signs `collect_fees` / `claim_position_fee` (Orca `collect_fees.rs` checks the position token account owner or delegate [SRC]). The venue's share goes to the venue's authority. CP-swap's creator share goes to `pool_state.pool_creator` [SRC].
- **F. Can an external custom program enforce a mandatory split?** Only of what it receives, by owning the right to collect (section 7).
- **G. Can our program atomically route 60/15/15/10?** Yes, within one instruction it can collect and transfer to four recipients, or it can accrue into program-owned vaults and let a permissionless instruction pay out (section 6). Both are atomic inside a transaction [KNOWN].
- **H. Can the venue bypass our program?** The venue's own admin actions do not pass through our program (fee changes, pause). Traders' swaps never pass through it either. What it cannot do is collect our position's fees without the NFT holder's signature, which is our PDA [SRC].
- **I. Can a trader interact directly with the underlying pool?** Yes, always.
- **J. Can the venue's fee recipient be changed?** The venue's own recipients (protocol owner, fund owner) can be changed by its admin (CP-swap `update_config` params 3 and 4 [SRC]). Our position's recipient cannot be changed by the venue.
- **K. Can an administrator change fee behavior?** Yes in every venue read. Orca: `set_fee_rate` and `set_protocol_fee_rate`, signed by the config's fee authority (`MAX_FEE_RATE` 60,000 of 1,000,000 = 6%; `MAX_PROTOCOL_FEE_RATE` 2,500 of 10,000 = 25%) [SRC]. DAMM v2: an operator with the `UpdatePoolFees` permission can change a static base fee, capped at `MAX_FEE_NUMERATOR_POST_UPDATE` = 100,000,000 (10% if the denominator is 1e9, to verify) [SRC]. CP-swap and CLMM: the hard-coded `crate::admin::ID` can change trade, protocol and fund rates for the shared `AmmConfig` [SRC]. DLMM: `update_base_fee_parameters` takes an operator signer [IDL].
- **L. Is there an upgrade authority?** Every deployed Solana program has one unless it was finalized [DOCS: solana.com/docs/programs/deploying]. **Who holds each venue's upgrade authority was not checked** (no RPC read was made). [UNKNOWN]
- **M. Can LP ownership be independently verified?** Position venues: read the position account and the NFT mint and token account owner. CP-swap: read LP mint holders. All are public account reads [SRC].
- **N. Can LP locking/burning be independently verified?** Orca: a `lock_config` PDA exists and the position NFT is frozen (Token-2022 freeze), and `decrease_liquidity` and `close_position_with_token_extensions` return `OperationNotAllowedOnLockedPosition` [SRC]. DAMM v2: `permanent_locked_liquidity` is a field of both the position and the pool [SRC]. CP-swap: a burn is visible as a lower LP supply, but burning forfeits the fee claim [INFER]. A time-based lock was not found in Orca or DAMM v2 in the files read (DAMM v2 has `ix_lock_position` with a vesting schedule [SRC, not read in detail]).
- **O. Can Token Proof observe the relevant state from RPC?** Yes for everything above: all of it is account data. Observation must use `getAccountInfo` and decode with the venue's layout. [INFER from SRC.]
- **P. What accounts would verification need?** Section 9.

## 5. Fee-origin analysis

For a swap of `x` units of the input token:

1. The trader submits a venue swap (directly, or through an aggregator that calls the venue).
2. The venue's swap instruction computes `fee = f(x, fee_rate)`, with `fee_rate` read from pool or config state the venue's admin controls.
3. The venue credits that fee to its internal buckets, according to its design:
   - **Reserve-embedded (CP-swap):** the LP share of the fee simply stays in the vaults. The creator, protocol and fund shares are tracked in counters.
   - **Per-position counters (Orca, DAMM v2, CLMM):** a global fee-growth accumulator is updated; each position later reads its share. A protocol share is split off first.
4. Our program is never invoked.

Consequences:
- An external program can receive a fee only by **being the holder of the right to withdraw it**: the position NFT owner, the LP-token holder, or the venue's "creator" role.
- It cannot add a fee on top, and it cannot see or intercept the swap.
- Total fee, the protocol's take, and who else earns LP fees in the same pool are properties of the venue and of other liquidity providers.
- Fees arrive in **both pool tokens** (the input side of each trade): the new token and the quote token. The split must be applied to both assets, and recipients receive both unless a conversion step is added (a conversion is itself a swap, with price impact and a trust cost).
- In a custom AMM (Architecture C) the origin is our swap instruction. Our program computes the fee from constants, takes it from the input, and is the only writer of the fee path.

## 6. Custom-program architectures

Definitions and verdicts. "Enforced" means the property holds because of program code and account ownership that anyone can read and that no one but the stated authority can change.

### Architecture A: existing AMM plus an external fee-routing program

**Shape.** A splitter program, with a per-launch immutable config account: the four recipients and the 6000/1500/1500/1000 bps. A program-derived account owns the venue position (or the creator role). Fee collection and payout are one permissionless instruction: collect from the venue by CPI (the PDA signs), then pay or accrue per recipient. The liquidity position is permanently locked.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is 60/15/15/10 enforceable? | **Yes, for the amount the program collects**, provided the splitter's config is immutable and the program upgrade authority is revoked or governed. [INFER from SRC] |
| Can users bypass it? | They cannot bypass it on the locked pool: fees accrue regardless of who trades. They can trade on other pools, which bypasses the fee entirely. |
| Atomic? | Collect and pay in one instruction is atomic [KNOWN]. The venue swap that generated the fee is separate and earlier. |
| Can a failed destination transfer revert the swap? | No. The swap never calls our program. A failed payout reverts only the collect-and-pay instruction. Use accrual vaults with per-recipient withdraw so one bad recipient cannot block the other three. |
| Can one recipient be omitted? | Not if the payout instruction has the four recipients hard-wired (or read from the immutable config) and rejects any account that does not match. |
| Creator redirect their 60%? | Only if the config lets them. If the creator address is fixed at launch, no (but a lost key loses the money). If changeable, only the creator can change their own share. Product decision. |
| Charity / tax reserve / protocol destination changed? | Not if immutable. Whether they must be changeable (wallet compromise, charity suspension) is the open governance question. |
| Administrator change the split? | Not with an immutable config and no admin instruction. The venue admin can change the **venue fee**, which changes the amount, not the ratio. |
| Upgrade authority change the logic? | Yes, unless revoked. A revoked authority removes the ability to fix bugs or adapt to venue changes. |
| Recipient account unavailable? | A closed or frozen token account makes a direct transfer fail. Accrual vaults avoid this. |
| Venue changes its program? | Our CPI could break (layout or instruction changes). With the NFT in our PDA, only our program can sign for it, so a broken CPI strands the fees until our program is upgraded. This is a direct tension with immutability. |
| Our program upgraded? | If the upgrade authority is held: the logic is not immutable. Token Proof must display the authority and the "revoked / multisig / single key" state. |
| Immutable vs governed | Immutable: the recipient table, bps, program ID of the venue, the PDA seeds. Governed (if at all): upgrade authority, recipient rotation (if allowed). |
| What must Token Proof observe to call the split VERIFIED? | Section 9. |

### Architecture B: existing AMM with our program controlling the fee destination

**Finding: this collapses into A.** No venue read exposes a free-form per-pool "fee recipient" field that a non-admin can set. The roles that exist are: the position NFT holder (Orca, DAMM v2), the LP-token holder (CP-swap), `pool_creator` (CP-swap, only for permissioned pools), and the DBC `fee_claimer` and creator (transferable). Each is a role our PDA can occupy; none is a destination setting. The venue-level "recipients" (protocol owner, fund owner) are the venue's own and admin-settable [SRC]. So "our program controls the fee destination" means "our PDA is the position owner or creator", which is A. The answers above apply.

### Architecture C: our own pool/AMM program

**Shape.** A constant-product (or similar) pool whose swap instruction charges a fee from constants (or an immutable per-pool config) and writes it to program-owned fee vaults; a permissionless `distribute` pays the four recipients from the vaults per the immutable table. Liquidity has no withdraw instruction (or only a time or never unlock rule in code). Mint authority is already revoked by the supply model.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is 60/15/15/10 enforceable? | **Yes, on the full fee the pool charges**, with no third-party admin able to change the rate or take a share. |
| Can users bypass it? | Not on this pool. Anyone can still create other pools for the mint elsewhere (classic mint, permissionless venues), so a global token tax is not achievable. |
| Atomic? | Fee accrual is inside the swap instruction; atomic with the swap. |
| Failed destination revert the swap? | Only if payouts happen inside swap. Do not: accrue to vaults, pay in `distribute`. Then a frozen recipient account blocks only that recipient's withdrawal. |
| Omit a recipient / redirect / change destinations / admin change the split | Same as A: fixed by the immutable table; only governance decisions (rotation, upgrade authority) reopen these. |
| Upgrade authority | Ours. The only upgradeable surface is our program. If revoked, no one can fix an AMM bug. If held, the whole pool logic is mutable. |
| Venue changes / disappears | Not applicable. No dependency on a third-party program. |
| Cost | A full AMM is the most security-sensitive program in the stack (math, rounding, reentrancy-style invariants, donation handling). Needs an independent audit; no existing audit exists. Discoverability: wallets, explorers and aggregators will not show the pool unless they integrate it. [INFER] |

### Architecture D: existing venue plus a protocol-controlled wrapper/router

**Finding: not enforceable.** A router only taxes the trades that choose to go through it. Any trader or aggregator can call the underlying pool directly. The one in-venue restriction found (DLMM's pre-activation swap address) applies only before activation [IDL]. D is a UI promise, or at best a convenience route. Do not call D an enforcement mechanism.

## 8. LP ownership/locking analysis and liquidity economics

### 8.1 The 40% liquidity role
- The liquidity role is 40% of **intended** supply, minted on-chain. With intended 100,000,000: 40,000,000 tokens go to the pool; 8,000,000 to the creator; 52,000,000 are never minted.
- Against **minted** supply (48,000,000): the pool holds 83.33%, the creator holds 16.67%. A buyer sees the on-chain supply of 48,000,000. A "market cap" computed with the intended 100,000,000 overstates the circulating value by 108% (100/48). The UI and Token Proof must label which base is used. [INFER, arithmetic]
- Because the creator holds a fixed share outside the pool, the creator can sell into the pool or open a second pool elsewhere. Neither is preventable with a classic mint. Vesting/transferability is PENDING.

### 8.2 Quote asset
Not chosen here. What the evidence shows:
- Any quote token the chosen venue supports can work; the venues' supported-quote lists were not checked. The launch configuration's `initialLiquidityUsdc` presumes USDC. [SRC: fixtures in this repo]
- A quote token with a **freeze authority** gives its issuer the power to freeze any holder's token account [KNOWN, verify on-chain per mint]. If a recipient's or the pool's quote account were frozen, a direct payout would fail, and a vault holding the quote could be frozen. Wrapped SOL has no such issuer. This affects recipient design (accrual vaults, per-recipient withdraw).
- USD-denominated sizing ("50,000 USDC") needs no oracle only if the quote is the unit. With a SOL quote, a USD target needs a price source; this document assumes none is used.

### 8.3 Initial price and amount
- For a two-sided constant-product pool: initial price = quote deposited / tokens deposited. With intended supply `S` and the 40% role, tokens deposited = `0.40 S`. The quote amount `Q` is a product decision. The price is then `Q / (0.40 S)` in quote per token (adjusted for decimals).
- Concentrated-liquidity venues (Orca, CLMM, DAMM v2) accept **single-sided** deposits above the current price. That would allow a launch with tokens only and no quote funding, with buyers supplying the quote. It changes risk and price discovery. It is an option, not a recommendation.
- Who funds `Q` is a custody and money-movement decision: the creator's wallet (the approved fee payer model covers network and rent only) or someone else. PENDING.

### 8.4 Ownership and lock options (technical facts only)

| Option | Technically possible? | Verifiable on-chain? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creator holds the position/LP unlocked | yes | yes | Creator can remove liquidity: a rug path. Cannot support "liquidity locked". |
| Our PDA holds the position, no lock | yes | yes | Removal needs our program; if our program has a withdraw instruction, it is a governed power. |
| Our PDA holds the position, permanently locked | Orca and DAMM v2: yes [SRC] | yes (lock config / frozen NFT / `permanent_locked_liquidity`) | Liquidity cannot be removed by anyone; fees can still be collected. |
| LP tokens burned (CP-swap) | yes | yes (supply) | The LP fee share is forfeited (it stays in reserves). The creator-fee route is separate but unavailable permissionlessly [SRC]. |
| Time-based lock | venue-specific | partly | DAMM v2 has a vesting-style `ix_lock_position` [SRC, not read in depth]. `lockDays` in the launch config presumes this. |

Who may remove liquidity, ever, is a product decision; a permanent lock is the only option that makes "the liquidity can never be removed" a checkable statement.

### 8.5 Launch sequencing risk
Mint, pool creation, deposit, lock and mint-authority revocation are several transactions. If pool creation or deposit fails after the 40% has been minted, those tokens sit in whatever account received them, and the mint authority is still live until the revoke step. Minting the liquidity tokens directly into a program-controlled account (not a creator-controlled one) and revoking authority only after the lock is confirmed limits the exposure. The current plan's instruction order should be revisited when a venue is chosen. Not designed here.

## 9. Token Proof implications

### 9.1 What the current proof can check
`ChainObservation` today holds: network, mint address, decimals, supply, mint authority, freeze authority, metadata, `liquidity {initialLiquidityUsdc, lockDays}`, and `feeRouting {creatorBps, taxReserveBps, charityBps, protocolBps, creatorRecipient, taxReserveRecipient, charityRecipient}` (`packages/shared/src/proof.ts`). Existing checks: supply (now minted supply), mint and freeze authority, `FEE_SPLIT_MATCH`, recipient matches, `LIQUIDITY_CONFIGURATION_MATCH`.

### 9.2 What it cannot express
- **Where the numbers come from.** `feeRouting` bps and recipients are only meaningful if read from the **splitter config account's bytes** and the account's owner is the splitter program. A server-written value would be SERVER CONFIGURED, not observed. The schema does not carry the program ID or account owner.
- No field for: program ID(s), program upgrade authority, whether the config is immutable, position owner, lock state, pool reserves, venue fee parameters, pool status, quote mint, or the venue admin's powers.
- `lockDays: number | null` cannot represent "permanent" or "burned".
- `initialLiquidityUsdc` presumes a USDC quote and a creation-time amount; the chain shows current reserves, which move with every trade.

### 9.3 Required observations per architecture

| Observation | A (position venue) | C (own AMM) | Account(s) to read | Check type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Splitter program ID is the expected one | yes | n/a (the AMM and splitter are ours) | the config account's `owner` | equality |
| Splitter / AMM upgrade authority | yes | yes | program-data account of each program | `None` = immutable; address = disclosed governance |
| Recipient table and bps in config | yes | yes | config account data | equality with registry (charity), launch config (creator), decided destinations |
| Config immutability | yes | yes | absence of a modify instruction in the audited code; upgrade authority state | derived, disclosed |
| Venue program ID(s) | yes | n/a | pool account owner | equality with an allow-list |
| Venue pool is the expected pool for the mint pair | yes | yes | pool account | equality of mints and vaults |
| Position/LP owned by the splitter PDA | yes | n/a | position NFT token account owner; position account | equality |
| Position locked | yes | n/a | Orca `lock_config`, frozen NFT; DAMM v2 `permanent_locked_liquidity` | boolean |
| Liquidity amount (token side equals the 40% role) | yes | yes | vault balances | within tolerance, time-stamped |
| Venue fee rate and protocol take | yes | n/a | pool/config account | observed value, with "venue admin can change" disclosure |
| Pool status (swaps enabled) | yes | yes | pool state | enum |
| Venue admin / operator identity | yes | n/a | venue config account | disclosure only |
| Mint authority = None | yes | yes | mint account | existing check |
| Freeze authority = None | yes | yes | mint account | existing check |
| Supply = minted role sum | yes | yes | mint account | existing check |
| Charity / tax reserve / protocol destination | yes | yes | config account, plus the destination's own token accounts exist | equality |
| Quote mint | yes | yes | pool account | equality |
| Quote mint freeze authority | yes | yes | quote mint account | disclosure |

### 9.4 Can the existing architecture support it?
- **Mechanics yes, schema no.** The observation pipeline (append-only, hash-chained, RPC vs FIXTURE separation, `evaluateProof` as the single rule) can host new checks without change to its principles.
- **New checks will eventually be needed**, for example: program identity, upgrade authority, config immutability, position ownership, lock state, venue fee parameters, pool status, quote mint, liquidity-token-side match. `ObservedFeeRouting` must become a decoded view of a specific account, with its address and owner, and `lockDays` must widen to a lock descriptor. This is future work and is not started.
- A strict rule for the future: a fee-split check can be VERIFIED only when the values come from an account owned by the splitter program, decoded from raw bytes, and the upgrade authority state is itself observed and shown. A venue-fee check can only be OBSERVED, never VERIFIED as a guarantee, because the venue admin can change it.

## 10. Solana constraints

- **Transaction size.** 1232 bytes today [DOCS]. A larger size (SIMD-0296, 4096 bytes) is targeted for Q3 2026 per the Solana Foundation page; whether it is active is **unknown**. Plan for 1232. A claim-and-payout transaction touching the venue, four recipients and two assets is account-heavy, so address lookup tables (v0 transactions) are likely.
- **Accounts per transaction.** A documented lock limit of 64 [KNOWN]. A collect with two pool vaults, a position, an NFT account, four recipients times two assets, plus programs fits but is near the practical limit with all token programs.
- **Compute units.** Default 200k per instruction, 1.4M per transaction [KNOWN; the 1.4M figure was not found in an official page in this session]. **No venue CU cost was measured.** Measuring needs simulation against a cluster, which is out of scope.
- **CPI depth.** Maximum depth 4 under the current constant (`MAX_INSTRUCTION_STACK_DEPTH` = 5) [DOCS: cpi-execution]; a change to 8 (SIMD-0268) was mentioned and status is unknown. A splitter CPI-ing into a venue that CPIs into the token program uses depth 3. A router in front of a venue CPI uses the same depth. Not a blocker for A or C. It limits wrapping a venue behind a wrapper that itself is called by another program.
- **PDAs and signing.** A PDA can own positions and token accounts and sign only through its own program via `invoke_signed` [DOCS: CPI]. The splitter's PDA is the position owner, so no key exists that could collect fees outside the program.
- **Account ownership.** Only the owning program can modify an account's data [KNOWN]. The recipient table is safe from outsiders only because the config account is owned by the splitter and the splitter has no instruction to change it.
- **Rent.** Every new account (config, vaults, recipient token accounts) needs a rent-exempt balance read from the cluster. Not estimated.
- **Atomicity and revert.** An instruction failure reverts the whole transaction [KNOWN]. Hence: do not put multi-recipient payouts inside a path that other users depend on.
- **Upgrade authority.** Programs on the upgradeable loader can be upgraded by the authority; `--final` removes it permanently and irreversibly [DOCS: deploying]. Closing a program is also irreversible.
- **SPL Token classic compatibility.** Our mint is classic. Orca's lockable position and DAMM v2's position NFT are **Token-2022** tokens [SRC]. The splitter program must therefore speak both token programs even though the launch mint stays classic. This is a venue artifact, not a change to the approved `TOKEN_PROGRAM` decision, but the owner should note it.
- **Token-2022 (documented, not adopted).** Extensions could in principle do more: a transfer hook could invoke a program on every transfer including third-party pools; a transfer fee extension withholds a fee on every transfer; a permanent delegate gives a dangerous power. But Raydium CP-swap rejects transfer-hook mints by default [SRC `utils/token.rs:335-360`], and such a token would behave differently across venues and wallets. It would not make this split verifiable on any venue and it adds risk. No change to the decision is suggested by this research. If the owner later wants a token-wide fee, that is a separate decision reopening `TOKEN_PROGRAM` with evidence.

## 11. Security / trust analysis

Legend for the "basis" column: **ON-CHAIN ENFORCED** (code plus ownership), **SERVER CONFIGURED** (a row in our database), **UI PROMISED** (copy only), **UNVERIFIED** (not established).

### 11.1 Trust assumptions per architecture

| | A | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| Must trust | the venue's program and admin (fee rate, protocol take, pause); our splitter code; our upgrade authority state | our AMM and splitter code; our upgrade authority state | all of A's, plus users' choice to use the wrapper |
| Admin powers present | venue operator/fee authority; ours if upgradeable | ours if upgradeable | venue's |
| Custody assumptions | the splitter PDA holds the position and fee vaults | the program holds all pool funds | none beyond the venue's |
| Bypass paths | other pools; direct pool calls (for D) | other pools | direct pool calls, aggregators |

### 11.2 Scenarios

| Scenario | Effect | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Malicious creator holds the position (no program in between) | removes liquidity | prevented only if a permanent lock is in place (ON-CHAIN ENFORCED by the venue lock) |
| Malicious creator opens a competing pool on another venue | volume leaves the launch pool, so fee revenue falls | not preventable with a classic mint |
| Malicious creator redirects their 60% | possible only if the table is mutable by them | design choice |
| Malicious third-party LP | adds liquidity to the pool and dilutes our share of the pool's fee, or removes theirs | not preventable; changes revenue, not the ratio |
| Venue admin raises protocol take or lowers the fee | changes the amount reaching the splitter | UNVERIFIED as a guarantee; observable |
| Venue admin pauses swaps or withdrawals | stops revenue or exits (Raydium CP-swap: per-pool bits) | venue power [SRC] |
| Venue upgrades its program | our CPI may break, or fee rules change | UNVERIFIED (venue upgrade authorities were not checked) |
| Charity destination compromised or lost | funds go to the wrong or dead address forever if immutable | design trade-off; rotation needs governance |
| Tax reserve destination compromised | same | same |
| Protocol treasury compromised | same | same; a program-controlled multisig is the owner's stated preference |
| Our program upgraded maliciously | could redirect all collected fees | prevented only by revoked authority, else governance (multisig/timelock) |
| Oracle/price dependency | none for constant-product pools; a USD-sized target with a non-USD quote would need one | none assumed |
| Replay / double routing | payout must derive amounts from a vault balance or counter, never from caller input; an accounting checkpoint prevents paying the same fee twice | design requirement |
| Donations to the fee vault | would be split by the same ratios; totals can be inflated by anyone, so proof must compare ratios, not totals | design note |
| Partial execution | collect succeeds but payout fails, or the reverse | do both in one instruction; failure reverts both |
| Frozen recipient account (quote token with a freeze authority) | direct payout fails | use accrual and per-recipient withdraw |

## 12. Recommended architecture

**Recommended (for the owner to consider): Architecture A, on a position-based venue with a permanent lock.**
- A splitter program owns the liquidity position through a PDA. The position is permanently locked, so liquidity cannot be removed by the creator, by us, or by the venue.
- Fee collection is permissionless and pays into per-recipient accrual vaults; each recipient withdraws their own balance. The recipient table and bps are fixed per launch at creation. No admin instruction exists.
- Upgrade authority state is a stated, observable property: revoked, or held by a named multisig with a timelock. This research does not choose.
- The product statement becomes: "The 60/15/15/10 split of the fees this pool pays to the launch is enforced by an on-chain program anyone can read. The pool's total fee rate and the venue's own share are set by the venue's operator, and can change."

**Why it can actually enforce 60/15/15/10.** The only thing it enforces is the split of the funds the program itself withdraws. That is enforceable because (a) fee collection requires the position owner's signature [SRC Orca `collect_fees.rs`; DAMM v2 `ix_claim_position_fee.rs`], (b) the owner is a PDA only the splitter can sign for, and (c) the splitter's payout logic and table are not changeable once its authority is revoked or governed.

**Whether an existing AMM can satisfy the requirement.** It satisfies the **narrow** requirement ("the split of what the launch collects is enforced and verifiable"). It does **not** satisfy the **broad** one ("no one except our immutable code can change the fee or pause trading"), because the venue's operator can. The product owner must say which one "on-chain independent verification required" means.

**Is a custom AMM/pool required?** Only for the broad requirement.

**Is a custom fee-routing program sufficient, or must the swap live in our program?** Sufficient for the narrow requirement. For the broad one, the swap must live in our program (Architecture C), because only the program that executes the swap can fix the fee and the absence of a pause.

**Which venue?** The evidence does not make one mandatory. A shortlist that survives the source review: **Orca Whirlpool** and **Meteora DAMM v2**. Both allow permissionless pool creation, a PDA owner without a signing key, a permanent lock with fee claims intact, and both leave an operator able to change fees (Orca up to 6% rate and 25% protocol share by constant; DAMM v2 base fee capped at 10% post update, subject to verification). Selection criteria, none decided: whether concentrated or full-range liquidity is wanted, whether single-sided launch is wanted, fee asset handling, aggregator coverage, the venue's audit record and upgrade-authority custody (not yet checked). Raydium CP-swap is not recommended on the evidence (section 4.3). Raydium CLMM and Meteora DLMM were not evaluated deeply enough (DLMM is IDL only). Meteora DBC is a different, launch-curve product with its own mint and a two-way split; not evaluated for fit.

## 13. Runner-up

**Architecture C: a custom pool/AMM program.** Stronger on the broad requirement: the fee, the absence of a pause, and the absence of a third-party take are all in code we control, and the full fee is split, not just our position's share. Weaker because it introduces a new AMM as the most security-critical program in the system, it needs an independent audit and a time budget measured in months not weeks [INFER], it will not appear in aggregators or wallets until integrated, and it is the only architecture where a bug can lose all pooled funds. Choose C only if the broad requirement is non-negotiable.

D is rejected as an enforcement mechanism.
