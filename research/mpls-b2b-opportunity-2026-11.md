# Recurring B2B service, Minneapolis metro: opportunity screen (as of 2026-11-23)

## 1. Decision and confidence

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE. No candidate is a confirmed GO.**

- **Best candidate (conditional):** done-for-you annual **energy benchmarking compliance** for owners/managers of 50,000-99,999 sq ft multifamily and commercial buildings. It is the only one of three that passes every hard gate under your constraints (no license, no vehicle, ~$0 delivery cost, remote, scalable). The legal obligation is verified. **Paid demand is not.** Run the $100 test in section 8 before spending more.
- **Rejected as a business:** storefront sidewalk snow route (works as winter side cash, fails the "worthwhile recurring business" bar on ceiling and liability) and small-business bookkeeping (no buyer trigger, no credibility edge).
- **Confidence:** moderate that the ranking is right; low that benchmarking demand at $395/building exists. The strongest counter-evidence is a free state help desk (section 7).
- **Brutal comparator:** in the first 90 days, every candidate loses badly to a W-2 job. 50 h/wk at $16-20/h is about $10,300-12,900 gross in 90 days (assumed wage, not verified). Conservative 90-day net is $640 (benchmarking) or $720-980 (snow). Treat this as a side build, not a replacement for income.

### Bar used (set before scoring)
1. Passes every hard gate under: $1,000 cash, 50 h/wk, laptop + phone, transit only, no employees, no licenses.
2. Buyers identifiable from public sources, with a concrete trigger.
3. Conservative 90-day net cash >= $0, with <= $1,000 at risk.
4. Credible 12-month path to >= $1,500/month average gross.
5. Demand can be confirmed or killed with a test of <= $100.

## 4. Demand: confirmed vs hypothesized

**Confirmed (primary sources)**
- Legal obligation exists, with dates and a $1,000 fine power (state) [3]. Minneapolis runs its own program and list [9]. Saint Paul ended its ordinance and handed off to the state in 2025 [10].
- Utilities must supply aggregated data on request [3].

**Hypothesized (not verified)**
- That any MN owner pays a third party for benchmarking, at any price. No MN price evidence found. The only price data point was a Washington State University service at $4,000-15,000/yr for multi-property energy accounting [11], which is not comparable.
- That mid-size owners find it painful enough to pay $395 rather than use the free help desk.
- Size of the non-compliant pool. The state must publish compliance percentages by Dec 1 each year [3], but I could not reach the figure (lookup tool blocked).
- The Minneapolis $200 late fine came from a page summary, not verbatim text. Unverified.

## 6. Conservative first-90-day economics (2026-11-23 to 2027-02-21)

Calculations were run in Python. Inputs are labeled *est.* where they are my assumptions.

- Signed by day 90: 3 buildings *est.* Completed: 1 *est.* (data timing limits this).
- Cash in: 3 x $197.50 + 1 x $197.50 = **$790**. Costs: $100 test + $50 MN assumed-name filing *est., verify fee* = $150. **Net +$640.** Pessimistic (0 signed): **-$150**.
- Hours: ~204 (15 h/wk selling + delivery), so about **$3/h** in the window. The value is in the pipeline, not the 90 days.
- 12-month gross: 10 bldgs = $3,950; 30 = $11,850; 60 = $23,700. Year-2 renewals at 80% retention: $1,560 / $4,680 / $9,360. **Reaching $1,500/mo needs ~46 first-year buildings.**

- 7 clients avg x 6 events x $35 *est.* = **$1,470**. Costs: GL insurance at $45-132/mo x 3 [5][6] plus $255 gear *est.* plus $100 transit *est.* = $490-751. **Net $719-980** for about 64 h. Higher $/h than B in the window, but it stops in March and caps at ~15 clients.

## 7. Most powerful contrary evidence (against the pick)

1. **Free substitute from the regulator.** The state contracts Touchstone IQ to run a free Minnesota Benchmarking Help Desk, 9-5 weekdays, with phone appointments [4]. The statute requires Commerce to "provide technical assistance to owners" [3]. The tool is free [4]. You are selling convenience against a free, official alternative.
2. **High compliance without you.** Saint Paul reported a record 93% compliance (656 buildings) under its ordinance [10]. Most owners get it done themselves or through existing staff.
3. **Mature local market.** Minneapolis has run benchmarking since at least 2013 [22]. City-listed buildings already have Portfolio Manager set up, and repeat years are light work, which pushes renewal prices down.
4. **No paid-demand signal found.** No Minnesota price list or vendor directory for benchmarking turned up. Absence of evidence is not proof of no market, but it means demand is entirely unproven.
5. **Cash arrives late.** Most fees land Feb-May 2027.

**What would overturn the pick:** the test in section 8 fails, or the Dec 2026 state report shows Class 2 compliance above ~90%.

## 8. Test: $100 max, 3 weeks (Dec 1-19, 2026)

- **Offer:** "Done-for-you 2027 benchmarking (state and/or Minneapolis). I request your utility data, build or repair your Portfolio Manager record, clear Data Quality Checker flags, and submit. $395 first building, $195/yr after. $100 refundable deposit holds a slot."
- **Spend (<= $100):** domain + email about $20-30 *est.*; free Portfolio Manager account; free Google Voice number; up to $60 for a short LinkedIn/Google ads trial or printed one-pagers. Practice end to end on Portfolio Manager's test environment or sample data first (free).
- **Target list:** 60 owners/managers of 50-99k sq ft buildings: the 5 above, more from the city list [9], and suburban properties found via listing sites. Contact only through public contact forms and main lines. No guessed emails.
- **Script ask:** "Who handles your June 1 benchmarking? In-house, vendor, or not yet? What does it cost you in hours or dollars?" Log every answer.
- **Numeric rule:**
  - **PASS:** 3 or more distinct owners pay a $100+ deposit or sign at >= $300/building from <= 60 qualified contacts. Then continue and scale outreach to suburban Class 2 buildings in Jan-Mar.
  - **EXTEND ONCE:** exactly 2 deposits. Add 40 contacts, then apply the same rule (>= 3 cumulative).
  - **KILL:** 0-1 deposits after 60 contacts, or 70%+ of respondents say "in-house/free help desk is fine". Stop and refund deposits.
- **Secondary data to capture:** incumbent vendor names and prices (the first real price evidence).
